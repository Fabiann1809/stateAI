# Decision log

[Español](../decisions.md) · **English**

Decisions that [`SPEC.md`](../SPEC.md) (in Spanish) does not fully specify, recorded so they can be reviewed later. The most recent are at the end. References to `sensors.md`, `cycles.md` and `SPEC.md` point to the Spanish documents in `docs/`.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The domain uses 3 activation levels (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`) plus a restlessness flag. | Health Services does not offer RR intervals (see `sensors.md`). |
| 2 | Level names in the UI: **Enfocado** (`LOW`), **Normal** (`MEDIUM`), **Sobrecarga** (`HIGH`). | Chosen by the product owner. Screens and docs still present them as estimates. |
| 3 | The default target block for each category is the midpoint of the spec's range: deep work 75, study 40, reading 40, collaborative 45, other 45 min. | They are configurable constants and are learned per activity anyway. |
| 4 | Manual dependency injection with an `AppContainer`, no Hilt. | Lighter build and easier to read. |
| 5 | The category "sensitivity" of 6.3 is read as **detection sensitivity** (how easily the thresholds react). The vibration limit per hour is a separate value: deep work 2, study 3, reading 2, collaborative 4, other 3. | The spec table does not define the limit; lower limits protect the categories that need long uninterrupted blocks. |
| 6 | The haptic policy rules (event type, rate limit) live in `:core-domain`; `:haptics` only turns the events into Android vibrations. | Keeps the rules testable on the JVM and the Android module thin. |
| 7 | When the target block is reached, the "suggested pause" pattern plays once. | 6.2 has no pattern of its own for the end of a block; suggesting a pause is what the event means. |
| 8 | Until Room persistence arrives, activities live in an in-memory repository behind the domain interface. | Lets the picker work now without building persistence before its phase. |
| 9 | `minSdk` is 33 (Wear OS 4) instead of 30. | Wear OS 3 is outdated in 2026; API 31+ offers `VibratorManager`, and Wear OS 4 is where the Health Services sensor panel works for synthetic data. |
| 10 | Guided breathing is exempt from the vibration limits, like the start events. Exempt events do not count toward the limits. | The person starts the guided pause and it vibrates every few seconds; limiting it would break it. |
| 11 | `SensorSample` and the `SensorSource` interface live in `:core-domain`; `:sensors` holds the simulator and the Health Services adapter. | Same dependency rule as the repositories: the domain owns the interfaces it consumes, so feature computation stays testable on the JVM. |
| 12 | A sample carries a nullable heart rate and the magnitude of linear acceleration in m/s². | Health Services can skip seconds; linear acceleration removes gravity, so "movement" is comparable across wrist positions. |
| 13 | Segments store a per-window level trace (`L`/`M`/`H`/`-`), the minutes of the app's nudges and the means of calm and movement. | Phase 5 learning (cycles, focus profile, baseline, energy) needs per-minute detail; it is a derived summary, not the raw signal. Room migrates the schema automatically. |
| 14 | Cycle detection uses a Schuster periodogram with a permutation test that shifts sessions, removes session-bound effects and requires both halves of the history to agree. | Session start times repeat every day, so session-bound effects (like the end-of-block nudge) would look like cycles. Details and results in `cycles.md`. |
| 15 | The baseline is fitted with an exponential moving average (alpha 0.2) of heart rate over each session's calm windows. | "Calm during the session" is the closest thing to rest that can be observed without asking the person to sit still again. |
| 16 | An activity's learned block is the running mean of the duration of its valid sessions; its movement limit is 1.5 × its mean movement in clean windows. Both are blended with the category by `n/(n+5)`. | Simple, explainable and converges with few sessions. |
| 17 | Feedback moves an activity's sensitivity by ±0.02 (capped at ±0.2) only when it contradicts the estimate (bad after a mostly-focus session, good after a mostly-overload one). | Feedback labels a whole segment, so it can only adjust the overall sensitivity a little. |
| 18 | Estimated energy: 0.10/0.12/0.30 points per minute at LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH, +0.10 with restlessness, +6 for each pause that improved the state, ×0.8 / ×1.2 in learned good / bad hours. Low below 25. | Product decisions so that a full, varied day ends with about a quarter of the battery; documented as an indicator, not physiology. |
| 19 | The next-activity suggestion counts segments of the same weekday and hour twice, and those of the same hour once; it needs a score of 4. | Predicts routines from a few weeks of data without suggesting on thin evidence. |
| 20 | The ML evaluation trains on three synthetic users and tests on two it has not seen, against a Python version of the rules. The report gives the real results, which differ from what the spec expected. | Results: rules 0.52 accuracy (0.72 with the real resting heart rate), logistic regression 0.85. The single 2-minute calibration can land on a compromised minute and bias all later windows; see `ml-python/reports/classifier.md`. |
| 21 | `android.uniquePackageNames=false` in `gradle.properties`. | LiteRT 2.x publishes `litert` and `litert-api` with the same namespace, which AGP 9 rejects by default; with the check off it only warns. Review when LiteRT fixes its packaging. |
| 22 | The classifier is chosen at build time (`-Pstateai.classifier=rules\|model`); the rules remain the default. | The model is a pipeline demonstration trained on synthetic data; the rules remain the reference behavior. |
| 23 | The model decides the level only when its top probability is at least 0.5; otherwise, or if it throws, returns non-finite values or fails to load, the rules decide. | With 3 levels chance is 1/3; 0.5 means the model clearly prefers one level. The rules are always computed anyway, for restlessness. |
| 24 | The end-to-end simulation uses a closed-loop version of the synthetic user: each minute of work adds effort (double under strain), each minute of pause subtracts 8, and when resuming the state is redrawn. Days are four 90-minute periods; the fixed timer is 25/5. | The offline generator cannot react to pauses. The recovery rules decide how much pausing helps, so the results describe only this simulated world. |
| 25 | In the end-to-end simulation the person follows a played nudge (suggested pause or overload alert) 80 % of the time, and stateAI's baseline is a calm 2-minute calibration held all day. | The watch suggests and never forces, so full compliance would overstate it; a still calibration is what the watch asks for on first use. |
| 26 | The UI shows a fourth state, **Agotado**, derived from `HIGH` plus restlessness or low estimated energy (`DisplayState`). The classifier keeps 3 levels. | The design asks for 4 states; without RR intervals a real fourth class would not be reliable, so it is presented as a combination of signals the watch already estimates. |
| 27 | Words for energy: "high" from 60 %, "low" exactly when energy is low (below 25 %, decision 18), "medium" in between. | The design suggests 30 % for "low"; tying it to the existing low threshold makes the word, the pink outline and the low-energy suggestion agree. |
| 28 | Summaries of past sessions show three states (Concentrado, Normal, Sobrecarga) plus "no data"; "Agotado" is only shown live. | The per-minute trace stores only the classifier level; adding restlessness and energy would change the stored data for a presentation detail. |
| 29 | Voice matching: names are compared in normalized form; "similar" means an edit similarity ≥ 0.8 or that all the words of a name (of 4 or more letters) appear in the other. Only within the same category when the category was understood. A new activity is created only with a recognized verb and confidence ≥ 0.6; otherwise "New activity" opens prefilled. | Typos and partial names should find the existing activity; a doubtful request must never create an activity without warning. |
| 30 | Voice input is chosen on each tap: on-device recognizer with Spanish installed; otherwise the system recognizer after consent; otherwise typed text in debug builds; otherwise the list. Listening is cut at 12 s; the confirmation starts the session after 3 s unless the person taps Change or Cancel. | Private option first, cloud only with consent, and the emulator (no recognizer) stays testable; a short countdown makes voice faster than tapping without starting by surprise. |
| 31 | Every pause nudge also shows on screen as a card with the reason: overload and leaving a long focus stretch suggest **Respirar** (guided pause); restlessness, end of block and long session suggest **Descansar** (5 min countdown, counts as a pause for recovery). The card only suggests: "Ahora no" or ignoring it changes nothing and it disappears after 5 min. Past the target block, a pause is reminded every 20 min, always under the vibration limits. | Vibration alone does not say why; the person decides with a tap. Recommend without imposing (SPEC 2) and do not leave very long sessions with no reminder at all. |
| 32 | "Foco" and "Enfocado" become **Concentración** and **Concentrado** throughout the UI (replaces the name of `LOW` in decision 2). All screens share one background: a very dark turquoise that fades evenly toward the edge (pure black in ambient mode). | "Foco" was not understood; "concentración" is an everyday word. A single background gives a less opaque app without saturating it and without changes between screens. |
| 33 | At the end of a session a personal rating from **1 to 5** (very bad … very good) is requested instead of three faces. It is a separate signal from the activation estimated by the watch: neither replaces the other. The stored names (`BAD`, `OKAY`, `GOOD`) keep their meaning and `VERY_BAD` and `VERY_GOOD` are added, so no database migration is needed. For the sensitivity adjustment, 1–2 counts as negative and 4–5 as positive; 3 changes nothing. | "Calm" is not the same as "it went well". With the person's own rating, the estimate can be compared with their experience, without claiming to measure performance. |
| 34 | The app learns which pause suggestions the person takes. Each suggestion ends in one of three responses: accepted, "Ahora no" or ignored (replaced by another or the session ends with no answer). Per reason (restlessness, leaving a long concentration stretch, end of block, long session) it stores how many were shown and how many accepted. With 6 or more shown and under 20 % accepted, that reason stops nudging and vibrating, except one time in 5 so the person can change their mind. The overload alert is never silenced. | Fewer nudges the person does not want, without imposing or inventing new suggestions. Overload speaks about load, not about taking a break, and must not depend on having been accepted before. Everything stays on the watch. |
| 35 | The summary includes a **Discovery** page that compares the person's 1 to 5 ratings by time of day the session started (morning 5–11, afternoon 12–17, night 18–4), within the same activity category. It uses only rated sessions from the last 4 weeks; it does not use the watch's estimate. To claim anything, each side needs at least 3 sessions on at least 2 different days and a mean difference of 0.5 or more. Confidence is "provisional", or "moderate" with 6 or more sessions per side and a difference of 1 or more. If not met, it says "I don't know enough yet" or "no clear difference". | Let the person discover something about themselves without claiming to measure performance or the cause of the difference. The minimums keep a single good session from looking like a pattern. |
| 36 | The UI adopts the **Aurora** redesign: deep violet background with turquoise and violet glows (pure black in ambient), translucent surfaces, turquoise→violet accent on the main actions, Lexend typography and a new Lumi (a robot with a screen) drawn in `Canvas`. It replaces the single turquoise background of decision 32 and the flame mascot. | The user's design (`rediseñoState`) changes the whole visual identity. Drawing Lumi in `Canvas` allows blinking and breathing without three VectorDrawable layers per expression. |
